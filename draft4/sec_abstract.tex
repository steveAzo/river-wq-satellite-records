\begin{abstract}
Satellite links reach remote rivers but carry only a few one-way messages per day, and the server never learns which were lost. This study asked what a server can soundly decide about water-quality thresholds from whatever bytes arrive. A node sends an error-bounded record plus witnesses of hard cases: quantised daily extrema and a conductance rate pair. A three-valued rule answers exceeded, not exceeded, or undetermined, and is proved correct for any pattern of lost messages under stated conditions. On 35 sealed rivers (2022), all three pre-registered tests held. On unconfigured thresholds, witnesses decided 0.182 to 0.226 more hard cases than configured decision flags plus the record. Whole-record recovery fell by 0.026 to 0.082; the largest exceeded the pre-set limit of 0.08, and the design was adopted post hoc. No certain decision was wrong in the tested configurations. Flags remained better on their own thresholds. The evidence is trace-driven simulation.
\end{abstract}
