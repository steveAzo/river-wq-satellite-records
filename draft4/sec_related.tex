\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The study combines established ideas, and Table~\ref{tab:novelty} states for each line of work what it shares with this study and where it differs. The comparison is of capabilities and settings, not a claim that earlier work is deficient: most of these systems were designed for interactive networks or storage, where a missing value can be requested again.

\subsection{High-frequency water-quality records and their quality}
In-situ sensors record river chemistry every few minutes, which reveals storm responses, diel cycles, and short pollution pulses that monthly sampling misses \citep{rode2016}. Such records are useful only if their quality is known: automated quality assurance and control has become part of streaming environmental data management \citep{campbell2013}, and open software supports the visualisation, correction, and flagging of continuous records \citep{horsburgh2015}. Agencies grade records by accuracy; the USGS rates a continuous record as excellent when its readings lie within narrow limits of the reference \citep{wagner2006}. This study took those limits as the tolerance of the record and did not address sensor quality control, which remains upstream of the encoder.

\subsection{Error-bounded reduction of sensor records}
Sensor data reduction with guaranteed accuracy has two classical formulations. The first fixes a precision and minimises communication \citep{olston2003,deligiannakis2004}. The second fixes a budget and minimises error: buffered multi-signal history was compressed to a bandwidth budget with a maximum-error variant \citep{deligiannakis2007}. For a single signal, the fewest vertices of a piecewise-linear approximation within a maximum error can be found efficiently \citep{imai1988,orourke1981}, also online \citep{soroush2008}; swing and slide filters build such approximations online with a proved precision guarantee \citep{elmeleegy2009swing}, and bounded-error approximation has been restricted to endpoints on a coarse grid \citep{lin2019,lin2025}. Recent work preserves statistical features of the series, such as the autocorrelation function, within guaranteed bounds \citep{munizcuza2025cameo}, or supports analytics directly on a compressed base \citep{sun2025shrink}. These methods bound the error of every reading, which is what a threshold decision needs; they assume that what is sent arrives. This study used the minimum-number formulation as the encoder of the record and added witnesses and certificates around it.

\subsection{Monitoring under suppression, loss, and uncertainty}
The closest problem setting is the monitoring of sensor networks with reduced reporting. In temporal suppression a node reports only when its value leaves a band, and the sink infers the rest; constraint chaining extends this to spatial suppression \citep{silberstein2006conch}, and model-based schemes send a model and report only outliers, with user-controlled error bounds \citep{tulone2006paq} or with readings acquired only when a probabilistic model falls below a required confidence \citep{deshpande2004model}. Message loss breaks suppression, because a missing report may be a suppression or a failure; BaySail resolves that ambiguity with Bayesian inference over the suppression scheme and the process model, and returns posterior distributions \citep{silberstein2007baysail}. This study faces the same ambiguity on a one-way link and resolves it differently: erasure coding and a block identity in every message tell the server which information it holds, and the answers are deterministic and three-valued rather than probabilistic. In-network filtering for type-threshold functions such as the maximum forwards only the readings that can affect the function, trading communication against a bounded error \citep{riva2014filtering}; it shares the idea that only some readings settle a decision, in a network where the sink can ask. Growth codes raise the share of sensed data that survives node failures by coding data across neighbouring nodes \citep{kamra2006growth}; they maximise data recovered, not decisions certified.

Query processing over uncertain data answers with interval bounds and prunes values that cannot affect the answer \citep{cheng2003probabilistic}; sensor databases that store an interval instead of the exact value can return answers that are certain or only possible \citep{cheng2003managing}. The notion goes back to certain answers over incomplete databases, the answers that hold in every complete database consistent with what is known; a system of operators is called safe when no incorrect conclusion can be derived and complete when every valid conclusion can \citep{imielinski1984}. The three-valued certificates of this study are certain answers in that sense, over the set of records consistent with the received bytes: Proposition 1 states their safety (soundness), and Proposition 2 their completeness (exactness) without witnesses. Plato and its predecessor evaluate time-series expressions over segment-tree summaries of compressed series and return deterministic error intervals for aggregates \citep{brito2017platodb,lin2019plato}. Closest in mechanism, TREAT combines erasure coding with compression and uses the possible minimum and maximum of a value, computed from the bits that were retrieved, to filter predicates before download \citep{taurone2024treat}. These works establish that interval answers, certain answers, and possible-extremum filters are classical; they address storage, retrieval, or interactive networks.

\subsection{Priority encoding transmission and unequal protection}
Priority encoding transmission (PET) lets the sender assign each part of a message a priority, the fraction of the encoding packets that suffices to recover that part. Albanese et al. proved that no system can have a total encoding length below the girth of its priorities and stated that systems reaching the girth exist for a priority function that closely approximates the requested one \citep{albanese1996}; Boucheron and Salamatian related the approach to the capacity region of the broadcast erasure channel with degraded message sets \citep{boucheron2000pet}. The layered group of witnesses in the frame of this study is an instance of this idea with two priority levels, namely the record, which needs $K$ of $Q$ messages, and a witness group, which needs $k_w$ of $Q$. This study claims no coding result. PET decides how to protect parts that the sender has already defined; this study decides which parts of a river record to define and protect so that regulatory decisions can be certified.

\subsection{Event-triggered telemetry, satellite practice, and task-oriented communication}
Send-on-delta sampling transmits a value when it leaves a deadband around the last sent value, with the threshold chosen for a target transmission rate \citep{diazcacho2017sod}; it is the standard event-triggered baseline, and this study evaluated it in development and in the first confirmation, alone and inside the record-first erasure code, and, after the second confirmation, on the final frame (Section~\ref{sec:explore}). Direct-to-satellite IoT has been studied mainly at the medium-access layer \citep{capez2022,tondo2021,alvarez2022,afhamisis2022,rathi2025}, with packets treated as interchangeable. The closest operational practice comes from animal telemetry: satellite relay data loggers abstract each ocean profile to a fixed number of broken-stick points and send the messages pseudo-randomly through the one-way Argos system \citep{boehme2009,photopoulou2015}. That practice fixes the number of points rather than the accuracy or the decision. Goal-oriented and semantic communication transmit what a downstream task needs rather than the data themselves \citep{zhang2023semantic}, and age-of-information research optimises the timeliness or the task performance of the latest value \citep{yates2021,chen2021,talli2026}; this study shares that objective, for past threshold decisions, with deterministic guarantees instead of learned or average-case ones.

\subsection{Reproducible environmental software}
Environmental modelling has long asked that models and software be developed and evaluated transparently \citep{jakeman2006}, and computational hydrology has been criticised for results that cannot be reproduced \citep{hutton2016}. Recent work in this journal defined levels of reproducibility \citep{essawy2020,choi2021}. This study therefore separated development from confirmation, froze its software and hypotheses before the confirmation data were opened (in the second confirmation, before they were downloaded), and reports both of its confirmations, including the hypothesis that failed in the first and the changes made between them.

\subsection{Position of this study}
The contribution is a source-side, byte-budgeted integration of tolerance-bounded time-series reconstruction, targeted extremum witnesses, and unequal erasure protection, with a receiver that returns conditional three-valued certificates for environmental threshold queries over a one-way link. It is a systems contribution: no new coding result and no new theory of threshold queries is claimed. The searches behind this statement were general web searches (results from the United States) and the bibliographies of the sources found; IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library, Scopus, and Web of Science were not searched systematically, and the statement does not claim that no such combination exists.
