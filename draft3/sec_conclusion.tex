\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study changed the question for river water-quality telemetry over a one-way satellite link from how to deliver the whole record to what the server can soundly decide from what arrives. A certificate language turns each piece of received information into an interval and answers threshold, extremum, conductance-change, and rolling-mean queries with exceeded, not exceeded, or undetermined; the answers are sound when the intervals are valid, exact only without witnesses, and limited by the tolerance for readings that lie near a threshold.

In a pre-registered confirmation on 31 sealed rivers, eight of nine hypotheses held. No certified decision was wrong. Targeted witnesses raised the share of decided hard cases over a record alone by 0.30 to 0.32 at six messages per day and 30\% loss, and decided thresholds that nobody configured. Decision flags remained better for thresholds fixed at configuration time, and a hybrid that hosts witnesses in the spare slots of the flag design cost nothing and added 0.29 to 0.36 on unanticipated thresholds. The hypothesis on false certificates after a forged message that passes the 16-bit check failed at twelve messages per day, and a longer check is the remedy to test next.

The claim of this study is generality and soundness, not the best decisions for known thresholds, and the evidence is trace-driven simulation. Three steps would move it toward practice: a measurement of real channel loss and error statistics, an evaluation of a longer check or a message authentication code on a new cohort, and an outside review of the code and proofs. The software, the frozen plan, and every result file are in the project repository.
