\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Table~\ref{tab:novelty} summarises the lines of work that touch this problem and states what each provides and what it does not provide for a one-way, byte-limited link. Most sources were read in part (abstract, introduction, method, and text searches); Albanese et al. was read for its definitions and its lower-bound theorem, and none of the sources was read in full.

\subsection{High-frequency water-quality records and their quality}
In-situ sensors record river chemistry every few minutes, which reveals storm responses, diel cycles, and short pollution pulses that monthly sampling misses \citep{rode2016}. Such records are useful only if their quality is known: automated quality assurance and control has become part of streaming environmental data management \citep{campbell2013}, and open software supports the visualisation, correction, and flagging of continuous records \citep{horsburgh2015}. Agencies grade records by accuracy; the USGS rates a continuous record as excellent when its readings lie within narrow limits of the reference \citep{wagner2006}. This study took those limits as the tolerance of the record and did not address sensor quality control, which remains upstream of the encoder.

\subsection{Error-bounded reduction of sensor records}
Sensor data reduction with guaranteed accuracy has two classical formulations. The first fixes a precision and minimises communication \citep{olston2003,deligiannakis2004}. The second fixes a budget and minimises error: buffered multi-signal history was compressed to a bandwidth budget with a maximum-error variant \citep{deligiannakis2007}. For a single signal, the fewest vertices of a piecewise-linear approximation within a maximum error can be found efficiently \citep{imai1988,orourke1981}, also online \citep{soroush2008}, and bounded-error approximation has been restricted to endpoints on a coarse grid \citep{lin2019,lin2025}. Recent work preserves statistical features of the series, such as the autocorrelation function, within guaranteed bounds \citep{munizcuza2025cameo}, or supports analytics directly on a compressed base \citep{sun2025shrink}. These works assume that what is sent arrives, and none certifies threshold decisions. This study used the minimum-number formulation as the encoder of the record and added witnesses and certificates around it.

\subsection{Deterministic answers from compressed or uncertain data}
Query processing over uncertain data answers with interval bounds and prunes values that cannot affect the answer \citep{cheng2003probabilistic}. Plato and its predecessor evaluate time-series expressions over segment-tree summaries of compressed series and return deterministic error intervals for aggregates \citep{brito2017platodb,lin2019plato}. Closest in mechanism, TREAT combines erasure coding with compression and uses the possible minimum and maximum of a value, computed from the bits that were retrieved, to filter predicates before download \citep{taurone2024treat}. These works establish that interval answers and possible-extremum filters are classical. They address storage and retrieval, in which a missing fragment can be requested again; this study addresses a one-way link, in which the sender decides in advance what to protect and the receiver can never ask.

\subsection{Priority encoding transmission and unequal protection}
Priority encoding transmission (PET) lets the sender assign each part of a message a priority, the fraction of the encoding packets that suffices to recover that part. Albanese et al. proved that no system can have a total encoding length below the girth of its priorities and constructed systems that reach the girth for a priority function that closely approximates the requested one \citep{albanese1996}; Boucheron and Salamatian related the approach to the capacity region of the broadcast erasure channel with degraded message sets \citep{boucheron2000pet}. The layered group of witnesses in frame version 2 is an instance of this idea with two priority levels, namely the record, which needs $K$ of $Q$ messages, and a witness group, which needs $k_w$ of $Q$. This study claims no coding result. PET decides how to protect parts that the sender has already defined; it does not decide which parts of a river record would settle a regulatory decision, and it has no notion of what the receiver can conclude.

\subsection{Event-triggered telemetry and satellite practice}
Send-on-delta sampling transmits a value when it leaves a deadband around the last sent value, with the threshold chosen for a target transmission rate \citep{diazcacho2017sod}; it is the standard event-triggered baseline, and this study evaluated it, alone and inside the record-first erasure code. Direct-to-satellite IoT has been studied mainly at the medium-access layer \citep{capez2022,tondo2021,alvarez2022,afhamisis2022,rathi2025}, with packets treated as interchangeable. The closest operational practice comes from animal telemetry: satellite relay data loggers abstract each ocean profile to a fixed number of broken-stick points and send the messages pseudo-randomly through the one-way Argos system \citep{boehme2009,photopoulou2015}. That practice fixes the number of points rather than the accuracy or the decision.

\subsection{Goal-oriented communication, freshness, and reproducible environmental software}
Goal-oriented and age-of-information research optimises the timeliness or the task performance of the latest value \citep{yates2021,chen2021,talli2026}; it does not provide deterministic certificates of past decisions. Environmental modelling has long asked that models and software be developed and evaluated transparently \citep{jakeman2006}, and computational hydrology has been criticised for results that cannot be reproduced \citep{hutton2016}. Recent work in this journal defined levels of reproducibility \citep{essawy2020,choi2021}. This study therefore separated development from confirmation, froze its software and hypotheses before opening the confirmation data, and reports a failed hypothesis.

\subsection{Position of this study}
The only novelty statement that the searches support is limited in scope: a general web search limited to United States results, with the sources of Table~\ref{tab:novelty} opened in part, found no earlier statement of a source-side encoder for a one-way lossy satellite link that, under a byte quota, selects an error-bounded record and feature-extremum witnesses and returns three-valued threshold decisions whose soundness is proved from the received bytes. The official bibliographic indexes were not searched, and the statement does not claim that no such work exists. Table~\ref{tab:novelty} separates what was taken from earlier work from what was combined here.
