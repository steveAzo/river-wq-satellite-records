\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Continuous water-quality monitoring underpins the regulation of rivers that supply drinking water and receive pollution. Wireless sensor networks have made it possible to measure temperature, specific conductance, dissolved oxygen, and pH every few minutes at low cost \citep{adumanu2017}. Deployments in Ghana and Kosovo have shown that such systems work in practice, but they relied on cellular backhaul \citep{adumanu2020,ahmedi2022}. The rivers under the greatest pressure are often the furthest from that infrastructure. In Ghana, illegal small-scale gold mining has degraded rivers in forested catchments where terrestrial coverage is sparse \citep{duncan2020}.

Satellite Internet of Things (IoT) services now reach such sites with small, low-power terminals \citep{centenaro2021}. Their capacity, however, is sold in messages, not bandwidth. A typical module transmits one-way messages with a payload of 23 bytes at 400 bit/s and receives no acknowledgement \citep{kineis2023kim1}, and service plans offer a few to a few dozen messages per day. A four-parameter sensor sampling every 10 minutes produces approximately \rawBytes{} bytes per day, which would require approximately \rawMsgs{} such messages. The question for a monitoring agency is therefore not how to stream every reading, but what a credible record of the river costs, and how to deliver that record when messages are scarce and may be lost without notice.

This study addressed that question with a definition of credibility taken from regulatory practice. The USGS rates the accuracy of continuous water-quality records, and an excellent record keeps temperature within 0.2\,\textdegree C, specific conductance within 3\%, dissolved oxygen within 0.3\,mg/L or 5\%, and pH within 0.2 units \citep{wagner2006}. A day's record was called guaranteed when every value reconstructed at the server stayed within these limits. The study then asked four research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item RQ1: How many bytes per day does a guaranteed record require, and how does that need vary across sites, seasons, and parameters?
  \item RQ2: Which satellite message plans can carry the record when a node buffers data across days, and with what delay?
  \item RQ3: Which delivery design maximises the share of guaranteed days over a one-way link that loses an unknown share of messages, compared with the designs used in practice?
  \item RQ4: Can an adequate message plan be chosen from a short pilot record?
\end{enumerate}

Each element of the answer has antecedents. Minimum-size piecewise-linear approximations with a maximum-error bound are classical \citep{imai1988} and have online versions for sensor streams \citep{soroush2008}. Compressing buffered sensor history to a bandwidth budget, including a group leader that allocates more bandwidth to the node with the largest error, was proposed for multi-hop sensor networks \citep{deligiannakis2007}. Erasure codes and unequal protection of compressed data are standard tools \citep{reed1960,albanese1996}. Satellite-linked animal tags have long compressed ocean profiles into a few broken-stick points and resent them pseudo-randomly over the one-way Argos system \citep{boehme2009,photopoulou2015}. What has not been established is how these elements perform together for regulatory-grade environmental records on byte-priced, one-way satellite links, and what such a record costs on real rivers. The contributions of this study are the following:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A measurement of the cost of a USGS-excellent water-quality record on 39 rivers over two years: a median of \needMedConf{} bytes per day (\needShareConf{} of the raw readings), with its variation across sites, months, and parameters.
  \item A delivery design for one-way links that sends the minimum guaranteed set of values protected by an erasure code across messages, together with a server-side certification of which parts of the record are guaranteed, obtained without any feedback.
  \item A pre-registered comparison with current practice, tuned on 2023 and confirmed on a sealed 2024 year and on two low-cost deployments, in which all seven hypotheses held.
  \item Design guidance for agencies: the message plan that suffices under independent and bursty loss, and a pilot-based rule that selects it.
\end{itemize}
