\begin{abstract}
Many polluted rivers, such as those affected by illegal gold mining in Ghana, lie beyond terrestrial network coverage, and satellite Internet of Things services that reach them sell a small number of short, one-way messages per day. This study asked how much data a regulatory-grade water-quality record requires, and how such a record can be delivered over these links. A record was taken as guaranteed when every reconstructed value stayed within the United States Geological Survey (USGS) accuracy rating of an excellent record. On 39 USGS river sites, the fewest values that guaranteed a day of temperature, conductance, dissolved oxygen, and pH needed a median of \needMedConf{} bytes, or \needShareConf{} of the raw readings, and at most \needMsgMaxConf{} messages of 23 bytes. Because the satellite link returns no acknowledgements, the design that protected this minimal record with an erasure code across messages was compared with current practice in a pre-registered study: designs were tuned on 2023 data and confirmed on a sealed 2024 year. At six messages a day with 30\% message loss, the proposed design guaranteed \gCodeIndPct{} of site-days, against \gRelIndPct{} for the same record sent without protection and \gSrdlIndPct{} for the scheme used by satellite-linked animal tags; repetition of the record performed similarly to the code. With a buffer, six messages a day kept 90\% of days guaranteed within a week at \poolSixInd{} of \nSites{} sites, and a 28-day pilot selected an adequate message plan for \sizeSuff{} of sites. All seven pre-registered hypotheses held.
\end{abstract}
