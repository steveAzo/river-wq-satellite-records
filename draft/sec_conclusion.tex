\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study measured what a regulatory-grade water-quality record costs and how it can be delivered over the one-way, message-limited satellite links that reach remote rivers. On 39 USGS sites over two years, a record within the USGS excellent-record tolerance needed a median of \needMedConf{} bytes per day and at most \needMsgMaxConf{} messages of 23 bytes. Delivered without protection, such a record was guaranteed on only \gRelIndPct{} of days at a message loss of 30\%. Protected by an erasure code or by repetition, it was guaranteed on more than 80\% of days at the same loss with six messages per day, and on most sites within a week when a node buffered its data. A 28-day pilot was sufficient to choose the plan at \sizeSuff{} of sites. All seven pre-registered hypotheses held on a sealed confirmation year.

For agencies monitoring rivers such as those affected by illegal mining in Ghana, the result means that a credible continuous record does not require a high-capacity link. It requires the right bytes, protected against loss, on a plan sized from local data. Future work should measure message loss on operational satellite services, run the encoder on field hardware, and extend the guarantee to the event alarms that operators need in real time.
