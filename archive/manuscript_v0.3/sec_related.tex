\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{High-frequency water-quality records and their quality}
In-situ sensors now record river chemistry every few minutes, which reveals storm responses, diel cycles, and short pollution pulses that monthly sampling misses \citep{rode2016}. Such records are useful only if their quality is known: automated quality assurance and control has become part of streaming environmental data management \citep{campbell2013}, and open software supports the visualisation, correction, and flagging of continuous hydrologic and water-quality records \citep{horsburgh2015}. Agencies grade records by accuracy; the USGS rates a continuous record as excellent when its readings lie within narrow limits of the reference \citep{wagner2006}. Derived quantities such as stream metabolism estimates depend on the diel shape of dissolved oxygen \citep{appling2018}. This study took the USGS limits as tolerances and asked what it costs to deliver a record within them over a link with very little capacity, and which decisions such a record supports. It did not address sensor quality control, which remains upstream of the encoder.

\subsection{Error-bounded reduction of sensor records}
Sensor data reduction with guaranteed accuracy has two classical formulations. The first fixes a precision and minimises communication: per-source filters were adapted so that continuous queries met their precision with the fewest updates \citep{olston2003}, and error thresholds were redistributed within aggregation trees \citep{deligiannakis2004}. The second fixes a budget and minimises error: buffered multi-signal history was compressed to a bandwidth budget with a maximum-error variant and a leader that allocated bandwidth to the member with the largest error \citep{deligiannakis2007}. For a single signal, the fewest vertices of a piecewise-linear approximation within a maximum error can be found efficiently \citep{imai1988,orourke1981}, also online \citep{soroush2008}, and several signals can be archived jointly \citep{gandhi2009}. Lossy compression for energy-constrained sensors has been compared extensively \citep{zordan2014}, and storage-oriented work produced multi-resolution and progressive encodings \citep{ganesan2003,barbarioli2023}. Bounded-error piecewise-linear approximation has also been restricted to segment endpoints on a coarse grid, such that each endpoint is stored as a small integer \citep{lin2019,lin2025}. These works assume that what is sent arrives. This study used the minimum-number formulation with per-reading tolerances as its encoder and adapted it to what a one-way link requires: quantisation-exact bounds, self-contained days, and censored readings.

\subsection{Satellite IoT and one-way delivery}
Direct-to-satellite IoT has been studied mainly at the medium-access layer: constellation design for revisit time \citep{capez2022}, slotted ALOHA with erasures at several satellites \citep{tondo2021}, uplink transmission policies \citep{alvarez2022}, scheduling of LoRa uplinks to passing satellites \citep{afhamisis2022}, and message replication for reliability \citep{rathi2025}. These works treat packets as interchangeable, and terrestrial schemes for the bulk transfer of buffered data aim to deliver every byte \citep{abdelfadeel2020}. Erasure codes and fountain codes protect data against loss without feedback \citep{reed1960,luby2002,ho2006}, application-layer erasure coding has been tailored to delay-sensitive IoT uplinks with sporadic feedback \citep{chaubey2022}, and unequal protection of compressed data is standard \citep{albanese1996}. The closest operational practice comes from animal telemetry: conductivity-temperature-depth satellite relay data loggers abstract each ocean profile to a fixed number of broken-stick points and send the resulting messages pseudo-randomly from a buffer through the one-way Argos system \citep{boehme2009,photopoulou2015}. That practice fixes the number of points rather than the accuracy; it was included as a baseline in both its single-day and its multi-day form.

\subsection{Freshness, value of information, and reproducible environmental software}
Age-of-information research optimises how fresh the latest value of a source is \citep{yates2021}, including over random-access and satellite links \citep{chen2021,talli2026,ramachandran2026}. Environmental regulation needs the whole record, and a record within tolerance everywhere also holds the latest value within tolerance. Environmental modelling has long asked that models and software be developed and evaluated transparently \citep{jakeman2006}, and computational hydrology has been criticised for results that cannot be reproduced \citep{hutton2016}. Recent work in this journal has defined levels of reproducibility for modelling studies \citep{essawy2020} and combined data repositories, computational environments, and model interfaces to reach them \citep{choi2021}. This study therefore separated development from confirmation, froze its software before opening the confirmation data, and published every result file.

\subsection{Position of this study}
Table~\ref{tab:novelty} separates what this study took from earlier work, what it adapted, and what it contributed. The algorithms are established. The contribution lies in the problem formulation for one-way, message-priced links, in a certificate that the server can establish without feedback, in the evaluation on real rivers with a sealed confirmation cohort, and in the decision-oriented assessment.
